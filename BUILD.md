# Build guide — V2 inclined pocket disc

This builds one dispenser module for **one pill type**. The module is the variant that
the simulator ranked first (see [results/COMPARISON.md](results/COMPARISON.md) and the
replay viewer in `viewer/`). Everything below refers to files generated by
`python cad/variants/v2_inclined_disc/build.py`.

How it works, in one paragraph: a disc tilted 50° turns over a fixed back plate. Notches in
its rim are pockets for exactly one pill; the fixed hopper wall closes each notch's outer
side. Pills pool at the bottom of the hopper, drop into a notch as it passes, ride up, and
fall out through a hole in the back plate at the top while the disc is stopped. A 50 mm
duct carries the pill past the motor into the cup. Unseated pills slide back down because
the slope (tan 50° = 1.19) is twice the worst friction (0.6). The beam-break across the
duct confirms each pill; the firmware indexes one notch at a time until it sees one.

## 1. Parts list

### Printed parts (PLA, Bambu)

Pill-specific parts are in `cad/variants/v2_inclined_disc/<pill>/`, where `<pill>` is
`iron`, `small`, `caplet` or `cap0`. Print the set for the pill you are testing; the
**disc, back plate and hopper are matched to each other** (the back plate's exit hole and
the hopper's position depend on the disc thickness).

printability check used.

### Print settings (checked by `cad/check_printability.py`)

| Setting | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material | PLA (BOM: Bambu PLA / PLA Pure). Use **black or another opaque colour for the back plate** | light PLA lets IR through the duct walls and the beam-break never sees the pill (BOM Sheet 3 note) |
| Nozzle / layer | 0.4 mm / 0.16 mm (disc 0.1 mm) | notch walls are 1.3–2.4 mm; the lead-in chamfer is a 0.25 mm stair |
| Walls | 4 perimeters | the thinnest designed wall is 1.3 mm (disc web, 1st-percentile); 4 × 0.42 mm covers every wall |
| Infill | 25 % gyroid; disc and back plate 40 % | |
| Supports | **none** | every part passes the 45° overhang check (≤ 4 mm² unsupported) |
| Elephant-foot compensation | 0.15 mm | the disc's back face slides 0.4 mm over the back plate |
| Brim | 5 mm on side plates and hopper | tall, narrow footprint |

Do **not** scale in the slicer. All clearances (0.4 mm running gaps, 0.35 mm pill clearance
per side) are modelled in the CAD; scaling breaks them.

## 2. Before printing: measure

1. **Measure 10 of your pills** (calipers): diameter or length/width, thickness, mass.
   Put mean ± 3σ into `sim/pills.py` (nominal ± `tol`). The iron numbers there are an
   estimate (ASSUMPTIONS A2).
2. Regenerate: `python cad/variants/v2_inclined_disc/build.py <pill>`, then
   `python cad/check_printability.py cad/variants/v2_inclined_disc/<pill>/manifest.json`.
3. Rerun the sim for that pill before printing:
   `python -m sim.campaign v2_inclined_disc --pills <pill> --trials 200 --workers 4`.
   It must report zero jam / double / miss / lost.
4. Measure the gearmotor: gearbox cross-section (assumed 12 × 10 mm) and how far the
   D-shaft sticks out of the gearbox face (needs ≥ 9 mm: 3 mm plate + 0.4 mm gap + disc).
   If the gearbox is not 12 × 10, change `GEARBOX` in `build.py`.

## 3. Assembly (in order)

1. **Clean the disc.** Deburr the notch edges with a blade; the notch floors are the back
   plate, so the disc's back face must be flat — sand it on 400-grit on glass if it
   rocks.
2. **Test-fit the disc on the motor shaft** before mounting anything: the D-bore is a light
   press fit. If loose, one drop of CA on the flat after step 5.
3. **Motor into the back plate.** Push the gearbox into the socket on the back of the back
   plate, shaft through the centre hole. Lay the clamp strap across the gearbox and fix it
   with **2 × M2 × 6** into the two ears (they self-tap; stop when snug).
4. **Hopper onto the back plate.** Put the hopper flange on the front face (the relief
   bulge in the hopper wall goes to the **top**, over the exit hole) and bolt with
   **4 × M3 × 16 + washer + nut**. Turn the disc by hand later to check nothing rubs.
5. **Disc onto the shaft.** Lay the 0.4 mm feeler gauge on the back plate inside the
   hopper, press the disc onto the shaft until it touches the gauge, pull the gauge out.
   Spin by hand: it must turn freely with an even gap to the hopper wall all round.
6. **Cover.** Press the cover's lip into the top half of the hopper mouth (straight edge
   horizontal, closing the **upper** half); two drops of CA.
7. **Stand.** Glue the two side plates into the base slots (CA, square to the base). Bolt
   the back-plate tabs to the side plates with **4 × M3 × 16 + nut**. The disc must now be
   at 50° with the exit duct at the top pointing backwards.
8. **Cup** on the base under the end of the duct.
9. **Beam-break.** Push the 3 mm IR emitter and receiver into the two holes across the
   duct, 8 mm from the plate (they face each other). Route the wires down the side plate.
10. **Home switch.** Mount the roller-lever switch where the disc boss or a notch edge
    trips it once per revolution (see `docs/wiring.md` for the pin); set
    `MECH_HOME_OFFSET_DEG` in step 4.6 below.
11. **Electronics.** Wire per `docs/wiring.md` (diagram `docs/wiring.svg`) in the bring-up
    order of section (e) there: power first with no loads, then each 3.3 V part with its
    component test, motor last.

The default build is for the iron disc. For another pill add the flag in `platformio.ini`
under `[env:dispenser]`: `build_flags = -DMECH_PILL_SMALL` (or `MECH_PILL_CAPLET`,
`MECH_PILL_CAP0`). `include/mechanism.h` holds the timing the sim used: index one notch
pitch (90°, small 45°) at 25 rpm with 120 ms ramps, 600 ms dwell, up to two revolutions
per dispense.

**Home offset** (do once): after homing, the firmware parks a notch at
`MECH_HOME_OFFSET_DEG` past the switch edge. Jog until a notch is centred over the exit
hole (look through the cover from above), read the encoder angle on serial, put it in
`MECH_HOME_OFFSET_DEG` (edit `sim/export_firmware_constants.py` so it survives
regeneration), rebuild.

## 5. Test procedure (confirms the simulated result)

The simulator's claim for V2 is **0 jams, 0 doubles, 0 misses** over 200 randomised trials
per pill (600 dispenses per pill). Confirm it on the real build with the same protocol:

1. **Dry run:** empty hopper, 20 dispense commands. Expect 20 × "MISS/EMPTY" after two
   revolutions each, no stall, no grinding. Any stall here is mechanical (rub/bind), not
   pills: fix before continuing.
2. **Fill test.** For each pill type, repeat 10 rounds:
   - pour in a random number of pills (3 to 20; 3 to 30 for small tablets; 3 to 12 for
     caplets/capsules) through the open lower half of the hopper;
   - send 3 dispense commands; after each, count what is in the cup and empty it.
3. **Record every dispense** in the table below: outcome (ok / double / miss / jam / lost),
   number of index steps (from serial), and for a jam the exact place (notch at the
   bottom / sides / top exit / duct) with a photo.
4. **Pass criterion:** 30 consecutive OK dispenses per pill type with zero jams and
   zero doubles. A single jam or double fails the test; record where it happened and
   compare it with the simulator's jam-site list for that pill.
5. **Compare with the sim.** Also run the original team prototype (V0) through the same
   10 rounds with iron tablets and log jam locations: the sim predicts most jams at the
   deck's chamfered stripping edge and the floor-hole edge, many misses (the 8.8 mm slot
   liner is narrower than the tablet), and pills lost past the rotor rim.

### Still unproven in the real world

- Real friction, pill coating, static charge and pill dust (sim: rigid pills, μ 0.2–0.6).
- Print accuracy: the sim uses the as-designed 0.4 mm gaps; a print can be 0.1–0.2 mm
  tighter per side, and the disc can warp. Check step 3.5 carefully.
- Pill dimensions: only estimates until §2.1 is done.
- Load cell: not mounted in this revision; the firmware treats it as a secondary check
  (beam-break alone decides OK / double / miss).
- Long-term wear of the notch edges and dust build-up in the duct.
